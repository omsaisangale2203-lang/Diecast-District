01 / THE BASICS

What does rarity mean?

A diecast model is considered rare when it is difficult to find compared with other models. That can happen because fewer were made, fewer reached a particular market, a release was exclusive to a store or event, or collectors have already bought most of the available stock.

Important distinction

Rare does not automatically mean expensive, and expensive does not automatically mean rare. Condition, demand, availability, brand, release details and collector interest can all affect a model's market price.

02 / SPOT THE DIFFERENCE

Common types of variants.

A variant is a version of a model that differs from another release of the same casting or product. Differences can be small or immediately noticeable.

01

Colour variation

The same model is released in a different body colour or paint finish.

LOOK FOR: Body colour and finish
02

Wheel variation

A model may use a different wheel design, size or finish across releases.

LOOK FOR: Wheel style and colour
03

Tampo variation

Printed graphics, racing numbers, stripes, badges or other decoration may change.

LOOK FOR: Printed details
04

Packaging variation

The car may be the same while the card, box, artwork or regional packaging differs.

LOOK FOR: Card or box design
05

Production variation

Small factory or production differences can appear between batches. Not every difference is an intentional variant.

06

Exclusive release

A model may be distributed through a particular retailer, event, club or region.

Collector terminology can differ between brands and communities. Always check the specific model and release rather than relying on a label alone.

03 / HOT WHEELS EXAMPLE

Treasure Hunt vs. Super Treasure Hunt.

Hot Wheels collectors often use the terms TH and STH. They are related collector categories, but they are not the same thing.

TH

Treasure Hunt

A special Mainline release identified by the Treasure Hunt symbol or other identifying details for that year's release.

  • Part of the Treasure Hunt programme.
  • Identification details can change by year.
  • Check the official checklist for the specific release.
STH

Super Treasure Hunt

A special, harder-to-find version associated with selected Mainline castings. It commonly features premium-style paint and Real Riders wheels.

  • Often has Spectraflame-style paint.
  • Often features Real Riders rubber tyres.
  • Look for the correct TH symbol and year-specific identification.
Don't identify by appearance alone

Paint, wheels and symbols are useful clues, but release details can change. Use a reliable checklist for the correct year and market. A premium-looking car is not automatically an STH.

04 / COLLECTOR TERMINOLOGY

What is a Chase model?

A chase model is a specially designated, lower-availability version included within some brands' or product lines' releases. It is intended to be harder to find than the standard release.

CHASE

There is no universal chase formula.

Different manufacturers use different names, symbols, finishes, packaging and distribution methods. Some brands may use terms such as chase, limited edition or special edition differently.

Before calling a model a chase, check the manufacturer's announcement, product description or a trusted collector checklist for that exact series.

05 / MARKET AWARENESS

Does rarity decide value?

Rarity can influence demand, but it is only one part of a model's market value.

AvailabilityHow easy is the release to find?
DemandHow many collectors are looking for it?
ConditionIs the model and its packaging well preserved?
AuthenticityCan the release and its identifying details be verified?
PopularityIs the casting, brand or theme especially sought after?
Market timingCan prices change as interest and availability change?

Treat online asking prices as asking prices, not proof of what a model has actually sold for. Compare recent completed sales where possible and avoid buying only because someone calls a model rare.
