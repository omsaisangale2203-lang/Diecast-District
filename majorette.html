French roots. Worldwide miniatures.

Majorette is a French toy vehicle brand founded in 1964 in Lyon by Émile Véron. It became known for small die-cast vehicles, often featuring free-rolling wheels and compact designs inspired by real cars and commercial vehicles.

Over the decades, Majorette expanded into cars, emergency vehicles, construction machines, trucks, trailers, playsets and themed packs. Some models include features such as opening parts or suspension, depending on the casting and product family.

Majorette is now part of the Simba Dickie Group. This independent guide introduces the brand and its collecting basics; it is not an official catalogue. Product ranges, specifications and availability vary by year and region.

THE STORY

Six decades of miniature motoring.

1964

Majorette is founded

Émile Véron founded the company in Lyon, France. The brand began developing small metal toy vehicles and built its identity around compact die-cast miniatures.

1960s–70s

The miniature range expands

Majorette grew its selection of small vehicles, including passenger cars, commercial vehicles and models inspired by real manufacturers.

1970s–80s

International reach

The brand became widely distributed beyond France. Its vehicles and packaging became familiar to toy and model-car fans in many countries.

1990s

New ownership period

Majorette went through business changes and financial difficulties during this period. The brand later became part of the larger Simba Dickie Group.

2010s

Collector-oriented ranges

Majorette developed and promoted more themed and collector-oriented products alongside its familiar small vehicle range. Specific series vary by market and release year.

Today

A broad miniature garage

Majorette continues to offer cars, trucks, emergency vehicles, themed packs and play experiences. The current selection differs across countries.

History note

Majorette has changed ownership and product ranges over time. For vintage models, packaging, base markings, wheel styles and country-of-manufacture details can help identify an era, but should be checked against reliable references.

WHAT MAKES IT MAJORETTE?

A miniature world beyond just passenger cars.

01

Real-world inspiration

Majorette models often represent recognisable production vehicles, including cars, vans, buses, trucks and specialist vehicles.

02

Play-friendly design

Many small Majorette vehicles are designed to be rolled and played with. Durability and features depend on the specific product.

03

Opening features

Selected castings include opening doors, bonnets or other moving elements. Not every model has these features.

04

Wide subject range

Emergency fleets, construction vehicles, trucks and themed sets offer alternatives to collecting only sports cars.

Collector perspective

Majorette includes both play-oriented and collector-oriented products. Compare models within their own series and price category rather than assuming every casting has the same level of detail.

SIZE & PROPORTION

How big are Majorette models?

Many familiar Majorette passenger cars are commonly described as approximately 1:64 scale, but actual dimensions and scale can vary by casting and vehicle type. Some products are larger or designed around a particular play format.

A real bus or truck is much larger than a small passenger car. Even when miniatures are designed to a similar scale, the larger real vehicle will naturally produce a longer model.

Check the packaging or official product information for the exact scale when it is important to your collection. Do not assume every Majorette vehicle is exactly the same scale.

Scale reminder

At exact 1:64 scale, a 4,480 mm real car would be 70 mm long. The actual length of a Majorette model depends on its real-world subject and whether it is made to exact scale.

LOOK CLOSER

Details Majorette collectors compare.

Body shape

Look at the roofline, wheel arches, bumpers and overall silhouette. Compare the model with the real vehicle or reliable release photos.

Paint & decoration

Check the paint finish and printed graphics. Some releases use special colours, racing graphics or service-vehicle markings.

Wheels & suspension

Wheel designs can differ between castings and series. Some models include suspension features; check the individual product.

Opening parts

Selected models include opening doors, bonnets or other moving features. These are casting-specific, not standard across the range.

Base markings

The underside may show a model name, copyright year, country of manufacture or other production information.

Packaging eras

Card artwork and box designs have changed over time. Packaging can help with identification, but use more than one clue when dating a model.

SPECIAL RELEASES

Colour, theme & edition differences.

01

Colour versions

The same or similar casting may appear in different colours. A colour change alone does not automatically make a model rare.

02

Licensed liveries

Some models feature real manufacturer, service-fleet or motorsport graphics. Check the exact vehicle or team represented.

03

Themed assortments

Special packs may group vehicles around emergency services, construction, tuning or another theme. Contents vary by release.

04

Vintage variations

Older models may differ in wheels, base markings, paint, tampo or packaging. Research the exact casting and production period.

Rarity vs value

Age, unusual colour or special packaging does not automatically prove rarity. Production information, condition, authenticity, demand and completed-sale prices all matter.

START YOUR COLLECTION

A simple Majorette beginner guide.

01

Choose a theme

Start with road cars, French vehicles, emergency fleets, construction machines, trucks or a favourite manufacturer.

02

Find your preferred style

Decide whether you enjoy play-friendly basic models, special themed editions or collector-oriented releases.

03

Identify the casting

Read the model name and check the base. Note the colour, wheels, decoration and any opening features.

04

Inspect condition

Check paint, windows, wheels, axles, moving parts and packaging. Ask for clear photos when buying online.

Set a budget

Choose a comfortable spending limit. You can build a satisfying collection without chasing every release.

06

Write down model names, series, purchase dates and prices. Photograph unusual variations for future reference.

BUY SMART

A quick buying checklist.

01

Check the exact model

Confirm the vehicle name, series and product details. Similar castings can have different releases.

02

Look at condition

Ask for photos of all sides, the base, wheels and packaging. Check for paint wear or missing parts.

03

Compare sellers

Prefer reputable hobby shops and sellers with clear return policies and transparent product descriptions.

04

Research the price

Compare several listings and completed sales where possible. Asking prices alone do not establish market value.

Authenticity reminder

This guide cannot authenticate an individual model. For uncertain purchases, request more photographs and compare with reliable product references.

KEEP YOUR MODELS LOOKING GOOD

Store, protect & display.

01

Avoid direct sunlight

Long exposure to direct sunlight can affect paint and packaging. Choose a display spot away from strong light.

02

Reduce dust

Display cases and closed cabinets help reduce dust. Use a soft, clean cloth or gentle brush when needed.

03

Handle moving parts gently

Do not force doors, bonnets or suspension features. Use moving parts only as intended for the model.

04

Store packaging safely

Keep cards and boxes dry, flat and away from crushing pressure if you want to preserve them.

QUICK ANSWERS

Collector FAQs.

When was Majorette founded?

Majorette was founded in 1964 in Lyon, France, by Émile Véron.

Who owns Majorette today?

Majorette is part of the Simba Dickie Group.

Are Majorette cars 1:64 scale?

Many familiar small Majorette cars are commonly described as approximately 1:64, but exact scale and dimensions can vary by casting and product type.

Do all Majorette models have opening parts?

No. Opening doors, bonnets and other features depend on the individual model.

Does Majorette make trucks and emergency vehicles?

Yes. The brand's wider range has included trucks, buses, construction machines and emergency-service vehicles as well as passenger cars.

Are vintage Majorette cars valuable?

Some collectors seek particular vintage models and variations, but age alone does not determine value. Condition, rarity, authenticity and demand matter.
